What is This Workshop?

You will learn how to create handmade monoprints using oil pastels and objects, then digitize and develop them in Photoshop to use as textures in your Term 2 fashion design project.

Why learn monoprint techniques?

  • Combine traditional craft with digital design
  • Develop assets that strengthen your project's visual identity
  • Build a library of textures you can reuse and adapt

HOMEWORK: Create Your Physical Monoprints

Do this before class. Bring your scanned files on the day.

Materials You Need:
  • Tracing paper
  • Oil pastels (black works best)
  • White paper
  • Pencils
  • Objects related to your project to use as stencils (leaves, string, lace, mesh, etc.)

📺 Watch This Video Before You Start

Monoprint Process: 4 Simple Steps

1
Color the Tracing Paper
  • Take a sheet of tracing paper
  • Cover one side fully with black oil pastel
  • Build up an even, dense layer
2
Add Objects for Texture
  • Place stencil materials related to your project on plain white paper
  • Lay the tracing paper on top — pastel side down
  • Hold or tape lightly so it doesn't shift
3
Rub to Transfer
  • Use a pencil to rub firmly over the back of the tracing paper
  • Press hard to transfer the pastel
  • Cover all areas evenly

💡 Tip

  • Try a spoon or smooth tool for more even pressure
  • Vary the pressure to create lighter and darker areas
4
Reveal Your Print
  • Carefully lift the tracing paper
  • Remove the stencil objects
  • Your monoprint is now on the white paper

Troubleshooting

If Your Print is Too Light
  • Apply more oil pastel to the tracing paper
  • Press harder when rubbing with the pencil
  • Try using a spoon for more even pressure
Paper Issues
  • Use smooth white paper for best results
  • Make sure tracing paper is thin enough
  • Tape paper down to prevent shifting
Before class: Scan or photograph your prints at 300 DPI or higher and save as JPG or PNG files. Bring them on a USB or upload to your cloud storage.

TASK 1: Digitize Your Prints

Bring your physical monoprints into Photoshop and prepare them for editing.

Key Photoshop Terms You'll Use Today:
  • Threshold — Converts images to pure black and white (no greys)
  • Layer Mask — Hides parts of an image without deleting them
  • Blend Modes — Different ways layers interact with each other
  • Magic Wand — Selection tool that selects similar coloured areas
Scanning Tips
  1. Set scanner to 300 DPI or higher
  2. Scan as a colour image (even if black/white)
  3. Make sure the print lies flat on the scanner
  4. Save as JPG, PNG, or TIFF

💡 No Scanner?

  • Use a phone camera in good, even lighting
  • Hold the camera directly above the print
  • Avoid shadows across the paper
Photoshop Setup
  1. Open Photoshop
  2. Create a new A3 landscape document at 300 DPI
  3. Import your scan: File → Place Embedded
  4. Save a copy as PSD to preserve all layers
Always work non-destructively: Keep a backup of your original scan and work on a duplicate layer. Save versions as you go.

TASK 2: Threshold & Select White Areas

Convert your print to high-contrast black and white, then remove the white background.

Why use Threshold?
  • Removes greys for a clean, graphic texture
  • Makes it easier to select and delete white areas
  • Creates strong contrast suitable for fashion design layouts

Processing Your Print in Photoshop

1
Apply Threshold

Convert your image to pure black and white:

  1. Go to Image → Adjustments → Threshold
  2. Move the slider until you like the contrast
  3. Click OK to apply
2
Select White Areas

Select the white background to remove it:

  1. Use the Rectangular Marquee tool M
  2. Draw a selection around a white area
  3. Go to Select → Similar
3
Remove White Areas

Create transparency by removing the white:

  1. With white areas selected, press Delete
  2. This removes white, leaving only your texture
  3. Repeat for any remaining white pockets if needed
  4. Deselect with Cmd/Ctrl + D

Selection Tool Options

Magic Wand Tool

Best for selecting similar coloured areas:

  1. Press W
  2. Set Tolerance to 20–40 in the Options bar
  3. Click on the area you want to select
  4. Shift + Click to add
  5. Alt/Option + Click to subtract
Polygonal Lasso Tool

Best for selecting shapes with straight edges:

  1. Press L then toggle to Polygonal Lasso
  2. Click points around the object edge
  3. Connect back to start to close the selection
  4. Copy: Cmd/Ctrl + C
  5. Paste: Cmd/Ctrl + V

💡 Tips

  • Hold Shift to add multiple areas to your selection
  • Go to Select → Modify → Feather to soften selection edges
  • Always deselect with Cmd/Ctrl + D when finished

TASK 3: Layer Masks & Blend Modes

Refine your texture with masks and blend it into your design.

📺 Working with Digital Masks

Enhancing Your Texture

1
Add a Layer Mask

Use a mask to control which parts of the texture appear:

  1. Click the Add Layer Mask button at the bottom of the Layers panel
  2. Use a black brush to hide parts of the texture
  3. Use a white brush to reveal them again
2
Try Different Blend Modes

In the Layers panel, experiment with how your texture interacts with backgrounds:

  • Multiply — Darkens underlying layers; great for placing texture onto light backgrounds
  • Overlay — Adds contrast while keeping details
  • Screen — Lightens underlying layers; useful for layering light textures
  • Soft Light — Gentle contrast, good for subtle layering

Adjust the layer's opacity slider to control intensity.

3
Add Colour to Your Texture

Bring your black texture to life with colour:

  1. Go to Image → Adjustments → Hue/Saturation
  2. Tick Colorize
  3. Adjust Hue, Saturation, and Lightness sliders

💡 Tip

  • Try a fill layer underneath set to a project colour for a quick recolour
  • Use blend modes on the texture above to integrate the two layers

TASK 4: Apply to Your Project & Submit

Incorporate your developed texture into your Term 2 design and upload your work.

Where to use your monoprint texture:
  • Backgrounds — Add depth behind figures or product layouts
  • Surface treatments — Apply onto fabric or garment renders
  • Layered design — Build collage-style fashion sheets
  • Accent details — Add textural interest to clean designs

Bringing It All Together

1
Copy Your Processed Texture
  • Drag your texture layer into your Term 2 project file, or
  • Use Cmd/Ctrl + C then Cmd/Ctrl + V to paste it into your project
2
Position and Scale
  • Use Cmd/Ctrl + T to transform
  • Hold Shift while dragging corners to scale proportionally
  • Press Enter to confirm
3
Refine the Composition
  • Apply a blend mode to integrate the texture with your design
  • Use a layer mask to hide areas that don't work
  • Lower opacity if the texture feels too strong
  • Try different colours to match your project palette
